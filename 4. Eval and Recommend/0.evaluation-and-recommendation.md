# Evaluation and recommendation draft

15 October 2026 · Samuel's working position for the customer presentation

**I favor a gradual move out of Power Apps, using Devin to build suitable replacements while the existing tools continue to run.** I would make each migration earn its place through useful customization, acceptable ownership effort and a credible financial case. A full platform exit would follow those results and the customer's actual licensing terms.

## What my experience brings to the decision

From my experience at Pfizer and Marsh, I value giving people who understand a business problem the ability to shape its tools. Power Apps can give authorized makers that flexibility. But app creation is only part of the job. Design, data access, optimization, distribution and ownership determine whether the result is useful.

I have encountered planning and analytics tools where refreshing Excel-backed data took around ten minutes. That experience makes me ask where the delay actually sits before recommending a rewrite. Microsoft's guidance identifies Excel, queries, gateways and network conditions as possible performance constraints. A new frontend could inherit the same bottleneck. [Power Apps performance guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/canvas-apps/app-performance-considerations)

The most difficult knowledge to transfer is often why a field exists, which exceptions matter and who can authorize a decision. Devin can inspect implementation artifacts and help document or reconstruct behavior, but operators and subject matter experts still need to validate that meaning. A canvas app package also excludes resources such as connections and gateways; exporting the app is not a complete migration. [Canvas app export boundaries](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/canvas-apps/export-import-app)

## What this build changes

**All application code was produced entirely in Devin.ai using only two prompts. My Devin spend for building the three prototypes was $13.13.** That is concrete evidence that the agent cost of creating this bounded demonstration was low. Research, specification, human review and production operation are additional work.

The initial portal demonstrates three workflows sharing permissions, persistent state and audit history. The merged refinement adds an Access Reviews module, import/export and further controls. Its build record reports about 11 minutes for the initial build and 31 minutes for refinement, approximately 42 minutes in total, with 22 tests passing at that revision. Human time was not measured. These are Devin-recorded results; the application remains a synthetic prototype. [Versioned build evidence](https://github.com/samuel-zhang01/Cognition-Take-Home-Assessment/blob/eb505f3db3484e3dcf0f4ff2906b50562a8c32f5/3.%20Build/0.build-notes.md)

The useful signal is that one shared implementation can support several tools and a subsequent change. This makes Devin worth evaluating for the next ten tools. The build does not yet establish annual maintenance effort, production reliability or a comparative speedup over Power Apps.

## Three directions

| Direction | Why I would consider it | Tradeoff that decides it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Replace Power Apps fully with Devin-built applications** | Own the application code and runtime choices, with the possibility of removing eligible platform charges. | The customer takes responsibility for production operation, migration and continuing maintenance. This needs a funded owner and a complete exit case. |
| **2. Gradually move suitable tools out of Power Apps** | Preserve working tools while testing replacements, transferring business knowledge and measuring ownership effort. This is my preferred direction. | Running both systems adds cost and coordination. Partial migration may remove no licenses when the same users still need a retained app. |
| **3. Use Devin to build Power Apps code apps** | Gain a custom frontend while retaining Microsoft hosting, Entra integration, connectors and platform policies. | Retain applicable Power Apps licensing as well as Devin development costs, and work within the host's limits. A separate custom backend may still be needed. |

The hybrid can offer the best of both worlds when retained Microsoft capabilities are valuable, and the cost and constraints of both when they are not. I would consider it where existing integrations, governance or avoided migration work justify keeping the platform. Code apps provide control over the frontend within a managed runtime; they do not provide unrestricted hosting for any generated server. Compatibility with this FastAPI prototype has not been demonstrated. [Code apps capabilities and licensing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/overview), [runtime architecture](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/architecture)

Keeping and optimizing the existing Power Apps estate is the baseline against which I would test all three directions.

## Cost and engineering capacity

For Premium-licensed users, building more apps does not itself add another per-app base license charge. Additional users, storage, automation and other services can still increase cost. The customer's actual license mix matters, and 60 engineers is not the number of licensed operators. [Microsoft pricing and entitlements](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/products/power-apps/pricing/)

I would compare the Microsoft charges that can actually stop with the one-time transition cost and the incremental annual cost of owning the replacement. Those ownership costs include agent usage, human review and maintenance, support, hosting, security and recovery. Existing services common to both options should cancel out of the comparison.

The existing research illustrates the sensitivity:

The transition amounts below assume migration and production-readiness work, including knowledge transfer and data/control validation. They are not extrapolated from the $13.13 agent bill.

| Assumed annual spend removed | Assumed one-time transition | Assumed incremental annual ownership | Three-year net benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $250,000 | $100,000 | $100,000 | **+$350,000** |
| $250,000 | $200,000 | $200,000 | **−$50,000** |
| $100,000 | $100,000 | $100,000 | **−$100,000** |

These are scenarios, not quotes or forecasts. They use three years of savings from the start, before tax and discounting. Formula: three-year benefit = 3 × annual spend removed − transition cost − 3 × incremental annual ownership. If the first scenario loses its first year of savings while all three years of replacement costs remain, the benefit falls to $100,000. [Ownership model and assumptions](<../1. Research/0.research.md#5-build-economics-show-the-threshold-and-the-downside>)

Already-paid engineers may create no additional payroll expense for a small project, but their time still displaces other work. I would show cash spending and engineering capacity separately. The $13.13 result supports a low agent-cost claim for this build; an all-in replacement comparison needs the remaining costs.

## Maintenance and security

I expect Devin and automated review to help engineers investigate code and prepare fixes. I would test that expectation through a representative maintenance change. Power Apps already has Monitor, App Checker and Solution Checker, so the comparison is the effort and results achieved using each toolset. An expired credential, moved data source or departed owner still requires monitoring, authorized access and an accountable responder. Microsoft documents both orphaned-flow failures and remedies, including service-principal ownership for appropriate flows. [Power Platform diagnostics](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/architecture/key-concepts/performance/tools), [flow ownership](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-automate/change-cloud-flow-owner)

I would let business users express problems and request changes through Devin, with an accountable engineer reviewing releases. Access to repositories, credentials and production data should follow the task and its permissions. This applies to engineers as well as non-engineers. The prototype has not yet demonstrated independent maintenance by a new owner.

Custom code is not inherently more secure. Cognition itself recommends review and branch protections because generated code can contain insecure behavior. This prototype still needs enterprise identity, CSRF protection, protected evidence retention, recovery and operating arrangements before production use. Sensitive development inputs must also fit the customer's agreement; public Cognition terms restrict listed KYC and financial fields, with certain Enterprise exceptions requiring a separate written agreement. [Cognition security guidance](https://docs.devin.ai/admin/security), [Platform Terms section 4.2](https://cognition.com/legal/platform-terms-of-service), [Enterprise terms section 4.3](https://cognition.com/legal/enterprise-terms-of-service)

## Recommendation and next evidence

My recommendation is a staged exit, subject to evidence from a representative fintech workflow. I would first establish the optimized incumbent cost and renewal deadline, then choose a workflow where customization or delivery friction is material. Operations should validate its rules and outcomes; a named engineering owner should accept its maintenance and recovery needs.

The pilot should measure total human effort per accepted change, workflow performance and correctness, and the effort for another maintainer to make a change. Each proposed migration should identify either a charge that can be removed or a measurable operational improvement worth paying for. If those benefits do not justify dual operation and ownership, I would keep or optimize that tool.

User enthusiasm could help adoption: people often want to solve a problem without becoming programmers. I would invite their requirements and feedback while measuring whether tasks become easier. I would expect the VP to choose the direction that serves the company, taking engineering evidence about security, capacity and reliability seriously. Tool preference alone should not decide the outcome.

If initial delivery capacity is the constraint, scoped Cognition assistance could be considered if available and commercially justified. It would still need client review time and an accepted handover to funded maintainers.
