# Customer workflows, controls and outcome measures

Research checked 15 October 2026. Primary sources only. This is a supporting evidence note for `0.research.md`, not a claim that the hypothetical customer uses Stripe, Dataverse or a named flag provider. Product facts and analytical proposals are separated below. The Data `design-kpis` skill informed the proposed measures; no actual customer metrics or targets are available.

## Three customer KPIs to carry through the decision

These are proposed definitions for customer agreement, not externally established benchmarks.

| Primary KPI | Definition and calculation | Why it changes the decision | Measurement caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Realized net annual platform savings | Avoided Microsoft charges actually removed, less incremental recurring replacement cash spend. Report cumulative cash result separately: realized savings to date minus transition/build cash costs. | Establishes whether a rewrite delivers the promised cost result. | Invoice removal and renewal timing determine cash savings. Engineering capacity consumption needs a separate economic-cost line; already paid salaries are not automatically cash savings. |
| Human effort per accepted workflow change | Sum of active human specification, setup, prompting, implementation, review, correction, validation and release hours / independently accepted comparable change requests. Also report request-ready to accepted-release p50/p90 lead time. | Tests whether the next ten tools and subsequent modifications consume less scarce engineering capacity. | Agree task categories and acceptance criteria in advance. Report failed/abandoned work and rework; do not count only successful agent sessions. No fabricated speedup target. |
| Reliable completion of customer operations | Workflow-specific success/latency outcome below, together with quality and financial guardrails. | Prevents cost or speed gains from hiding delayed onboarding, erroneous refunds or failed production changes. | Do not combine unlike workflows into a reassuring portfolio average. The metric is customer/operation outcomes, not screens, commits or agent hours. |

Proposed owners: finance/engineering lead for savings and capacity; operations/risk owner for KYC; finance/support owner for refunds; service owner for feature flags. These roles need named people before production adoption. Compare on the same window, case mix, risk tier and maturity; record seasonal/staffing changes. Establish baselines before selecting numerical targets. A two-hour prototype can measure effort and acceptance, but cannot establish annual maintenance or business impact.

## Workflow outcome definitions and meaningful negative tests

### KYC review queue

Proposed primary operational measure: p50/p90 elapsed time from a case becoming review-ready to a valid final decision. Define review-ready and final decision once with the domain owner. Also expose unfinished case count and age, so faster closures do not hide an ageing backlog. Separate customer-document waiting time and provider processing from reviewer waiting and handling time.

Proposed guardrails: independently adjudicated decision-error rate = sampled decisions found incorrect / decisions independently sampled, segmented by risk and case type; evidence completeness = sampled final decisions with required actor, reason, policy/evidence version and decision time / sampled final decisions. Sampling and error definitions must come from the customer's quality process. A high approval rate is not itself success. Downstream activation can be observed but is not causally attributable to a UI change without a valid comparison.

Prototype test: two reviewers open the same case version; the first permitted transition succeeds and a stale conflicting transition is rejected. A viewer calling the endpoint directly must not mutate state or fetch forbidden evidence. Preserve the accepted transition across restart. These controls are not unique to custom software: Dataverse supports version-conditional operations with ETags and `If-Match`; a version mismatch produces HTTP 412. Source: [Dataverse conditional operations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/data-platform/webapi/perform-conditional-operations-using-web-api).

Provider evidence: Stripe Identity sessions distinguish completed verification checks (`verified`) from checks needing more input; they provide reports and redaction. Redaction can take up to four days and covers related reports/events/request logs, so evidence lifecycle is a separate integration concern. This establishes an example provider lifecycle, not the fintech's entire KYC decision or a compliance conclusion. Source: [Stripe Verification Sessions](https://docs.stripe.com/identity/verification-sessions).

An alternative to rebuilding evidence screens is material: Stripe explicitly recommends considering its controlled-access Identity Dashboard first to save development and retain sensitive evidence there. Programmatic access to sensitive results requires restricted keys; access after 48 hours requires additional IP restrictions. Document/selfie FileLinks expire within 30 seconds. Source: [Stripe access verification results](https://docs.stripe.com/identity/access-verification-results). Analytical implication: identify whether the needed app is a queue over an existing verification service, or truly a replacement of verification and evidence handling. Avoid copying raw documents into the prototype.

### Refunds dashboard

First determine whether this is read-only reporting, approval, or actual payment execution. For read-only use, proposed outcome = time to resolve a refund enquiry correctly, with data freshness and unresolved-state accuracy as guardrails. Do not burden a reporting-only scope with an invented payment engine.

For execution, report separately: approval-to-provider-acceptance latency; age and amount of unresolved approved requests; and end-to-end customer confirmation only where that confirmation can actually be observed. A provider success state is not universally evidence that the customer has seen the credit. Proposed guardrails: unauthorized/duplicate/over-amount executed operations (count and value); reconciliation coverage = authorized operations whose amount and provider state match the internal operation history / all authorized operations in the same cohort. Show immature/unresolved cohorts rather than excluding them.

Stripe example facts: multiple partial refunds are supported up to the original charge total; refunds can require action, remain pending, fail or be canceled; events cover their lifecycle. Stripe also provides refund execution in its Dashboard. A dashboard wrapper may therefore be removable if the customer's access and workflow needs are already covered. Source: [Stripe refund and cancel payments](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds).

Retry evidence: for API v1 Stripe saves the initial result after execution begins and replays it for the same key, including 500 responses; current documentation distinguishes API v2 retry semantics. Source: [Stripe idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests). Webhooks may be duplicated and delivered out of order; Stripe documents event-ID deduplication and signature verification. Source: [Stripe webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks).

Analytical test: simulate provider acceptance followed by a network timeout, retry with the same operation identity, then send duplicate and reordered webhooks. Require one authorized external operation, persistent intent, verified webhook processing, and reconciliation of uncertain states. A local DB rollback cannot undo an external payment. Use a synthetic provider in the prototype, with no production refund credential. Do not promise generic exactly-once external delivery from an idempotency header alone.

### Feature flag admin panel

Proposed outcome = p50/p95 time from accepted authorized change to observed evaluation of the intended value in the target runtime/environment. Track pending observations and observation coverage across specified services/regions/SDK versions. A sampled probe establishes observation there, not proof of global convergence. Guardrails: changes requiring rollback/remediation or causing an incident / applied changes, using an agreed failure definition; and unauthorized successful mutations (count).

OpenFeature's provider interface resolves flags and carries evaluation context. Its events include readiness, error, stale cached state and configuration changes. These support runtime monitoring; they do not constitute a full administration/approval backend or establish a delivery SLA. Sources: [OpenFeature provider](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/providers/), [OpenFeature events](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/events/).

Analytical test: accept a change while a runtime is disconnected; show desired and observed states without reporting propagation as complete. Include environment, actor, version, reason, fallback and stale indicators. A flag bypassing KYC or changing refund authority should receive the controls appropriate to that action. Reuse the existing flag provider where it already owns distribution and evaluation. For example, [LaunchDarkly approvals](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/releases/approvals/) documents permissions and approval requests; this is an existence proof, not a recommendation or assertion about the client.

## What Microsoft already supplies, and what remains owned

### Access and correctness

Dataverse provides record privileges, business-unit/team scope and column security. Effective role access is cumulative; broader roles can expand access. Security roles/profiles move through solutions, while teams/business units and user assignments need separate environment configuration. Source: [Dataverse ownership-based security](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/wp-security-cds). Microsoft explicitly warns against client-only filtering for security; user identification and protected filtering must occur on the server. Source: [Use SQL Server securely with Power Apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/canvas-apps/connections/sql-server-security).

Analytical implication: a custom replacement must reproduce the effective access policy and server-side business invariants, not just the visible app-sharing list. Equally, existing Power Apps correctness must be tested rather than presumed; the platform does not know the customer's refund authority or KYC policy.

Delegation claim in the draft is correct: nondelegable queries evaluate the first 500 records by default, configurable to 2,000. This is not a global source capacity limit. Query/data-source compatibility determines delegation; Microsoft recommends temporarily setting the row limit to one to reveal nondelegable behavior during testing. Source: [Canvas delegation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/maker/canvas-apps/delegation-overview). Customer consequence: a queue can silently omit matching work, distorting backlog and SLA measurements.

### Code apps as a third path

Current overview (updated 24 September 2026) supports custom frameworks, managed hosting, Entra, connectors, DLP, Conditional Access, quarantine, tenant isolation, B2B and health metrics. End users can use Premium, PAYG, App Pass or auto-claim. Limitations remain: no Secure Implicit Connections, no Power Platform Git integration, and no Power Apps for Windows; compiled assets use a public endpoint without IP restrictions, with Conditional Access the documented location-control route. Source: [Code apps overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/overview).

Code apps are single-page HTML/JavaScript/TypeScript applications. The host handles end-user authentication/loading; connector models/services and the client library mediate data access. Source: [Code apps architecture](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/architecture). Analytical implication: this is not arbitrary full-stack hosting for every generated backend. Compatibility must test the actual connector authentication and authoritative command service.

The absence of Power Platform Git integration does not prevent an ordinary source repository or CI/CD. Code apps can publish into solutions and use staged Power Platform pipelines. Source: [Code apps ALM](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/how-to/alm). Service-principal publishing of an existing app is documented, but requires environment access plus separately granted app edit access; the principal cannot grant itself that access. Source: [Service-principal publishing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/code-apps/how-to/use-service-principal). Devin compatibility remains untested and this path retains platform licensing.

### Connector governance has changed in 2026

Classic DLP is connector-oriented, with saved credentials held in connections; enforcement changes may propagate rather than apply instantly. Source: [Data policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/wp-data-loss-prevention). Microsoft's June 2026 [ACP announcement](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/blog/2026/06/04/advanced-connector-policies-are-generally-available/) describes advanced connector policies as GA, with allowlists and action-level blocking.

Current Learn is more specific: workload design-time enforcement is rolling out in order, and remains runtime-only until available for each portal. ACP currently supports certified connectors, not custom or HTTP connectors; classic DLP mixed mode remains relevant. Managed environments can block formerly nonblockable connectors; non-managed environments retain that limitation. Source: [Advanced connector policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/advanced-connector-policies). Do not turn a broad GA headline into a claim that every Power Apps authoring control is already available everywhere.

## Migration, recovery and retained evidence traps

| Verified fact | Customer KPI/cost implication | Primary source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pipelines transport solution/configuration artifacts; solutions do not contain Dataverse row data. Approvals and sequential promotion are supported. From October 2026, unmanaged pipeline targets face managed-environment enforcement notices and approval deadlines. | Budget separate data migration and validation; check environment/license consequences before describing deployment as included without conditions. | [Power Platform pipelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/pipelines) |
| Managed solutions cannot themselves be exported. Uninstalling one removes customizations and can delete data in included custom tables/columns. | Obtain unmanaged source plus data/evidence exports before retiring apps; uninstall is not a harmless UI cleanup. | [Solution concepts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/alm/solution-concepts-alm) |
| Backups default to seven days; eligible production managed environments can extend to 28. No offline DB backup download. Restore excludes audit logs by default; large restores can take up to 48 hours. Only solution-contained apps/flows are included. Canvas IDs change on restore; sharing with Everyone does not carry over. | Recovery-point/recovery-time assumptions require drills; preserve integration references and evidence deliberately. Backup existence alone does not meet the customer's recovery or exit needs. | [Back up and restore](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/backup-restore-environments) |
| Auditing requires environment/table/column configuration. Ordinary change auditing omits retrieves/exports; separate activity logging exists with licensing/production conditions. Privileged users can delete change history/logs. UI audit export is unsupported, but API/SDK retrieval exists. Large attribute values cap at 5 KB. | Inventory coverage and retained evidence, not just whether a history tab exists. Budget API retrieval, reconciliation and protected retention where required by the customer. | [Manage Dataverse auditing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/manage-dataverse-auditing) |

Retention documentation has a material nuance: the administrator article describes per-record stamping and new-policy behavior, but also a Security-page option affecting existing logs; the developer article describes `AuditRetentionPeriodV2` with a 30-day default, while the admin UI section lists Forever. Sources: [Manage Dataverse auditing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/manage-dataverse-auditing), [Configure auditing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-apps/developer/data-platform/auditing/configure). Do not assert a universal default or blanket retroactivity. Inspect the tenant's configuration and the control/API actually used.

Proposed migration measurements: required object/field/history coverage; old/new authorization matrix match; read-result and operation-state reconciliation; user task completion under a controlled pilot; actual cutover interruption; first successful recovery time; and residual license cost after migration. A safe cutover needs one authoritative writer per workflow. Rollback of the UI/database is not reversal of an already executed refund or accepted KYC decision.

## Scope and confidence

The public documentation establishes stated capabilities and constraints. It does not establish the customer's actual usage, licensed entitlements, provider contracts, restore performance, policy obligations, operating volumes, engineering effort or product outcomes. The synthetic prototype can demonstrate an endpoint denial, stale-update rejection, durable history, simulated retry behavior and a second module reusing controls. It cannot demonstrate production SSO, sanctions screening, real money movement, broad reliability or annual savings unless those are separately tested and measured.
