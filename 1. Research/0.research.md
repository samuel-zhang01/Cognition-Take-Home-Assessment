# Power Apps and Devin for a fintech’s internal tools

Research for the Cognition take-home assessment · **15 October 2026** · US list prices in USD unless stated otherwise

## Decision in one paragraph

**Research supports auditing and optimizing the existing platform spend, then testing a narrow Devin-built workflow before committing to a full replacement.** Devin can help create and maintain ordinary application code. The client would still own the resulting production service: access controls, financial correctness, evidence retention, recovery and incidents. The decision turns on **avoidable spend, accepted functionality per human hour, and operational outcomes**, rather than the number of screens generated. This is a provisional research conclusion; no customer invoice, production app or baseline KPI has been inspected. Subsequent prototype results are discussed in the [working evaluation](<../4. Eval and Recommend/0.evaluation-and-recommendation.md>).

The interview adds an important delivery question: **can Cognition/Devin relieve a constrained team's initial build workload while leaving a system the team can afford to maintain?** A timeboxed assisted engagement followed by internal ownership is worth evaluating, subject to an actual service offer, funded internal review/maintenance capacity and demonstrated handover. Sixty engineers is evidence of team size, not spare capacity. [Interview evidence and interpretation][I1]

The third interview adds **knowledge access and coordination**: helping non-engineers find supported answers about integrations and data paths could reduce interruptions, waiting and rework. Devin documents codebase Q&A through Ask Devin and Slack. This is a separate benefit to test, available even if Power Apps is retained; no time saving or migration ROI has been measured. ### Separate capability from availability

Assess relevant skills and funded time independently. A capable team with available time can test internal Devin delivery; a capable team without build time can consider scoped assistance. A team lacking relevant skills needs a learning and ownership plan even if agent capacity is abundant. If skills and capacity are both missing, a generated application alone does not establish an operable alternative. These are decision conditions, not findings about the 60 engineers.

Measure the work waiting on specification, domain decisions, code review and release approval. Devin can execute tasks while reviewers remain busy, so additional sessions may increase the review queue. Capacity relief is the reduction in **total internal effort for comparable accepted work**, including failures and rework, rather than agent runtime or nominal headcount. Keep named recurring maintenance capacity even if initial building is assisted.

### Knowledge access as a capacity hypothesis

The third interview describes a non-engineering user retrieving integration information through an internal assistant. Its technology is unspecified. For this fintech, test whether similar questions can be resolved using indexed repositories and approved connected sources, with citations and owner validation. Record active requester and engineer effort separately from elapsed waiting, and include incorrect, unresolved and escalated questions. A day of waiting is not a day of labor saved. Keep these diagnostics under the existing capacity measures rather than inventing a fourth primary KPI. [Evidence, source boundaries and proposed check][I3]

### Three primary measures for the VP of Engineering

These are **proposed measures**, not existing customer KPIs or promised targets.

| Measure | Definition and source | Decision it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Net ownership benefit** | Over the agreed horizon: avoidable incumbent spend minus incremental replacement operating cost minus transition cost. Procurement supplies actual avoidability; Finance values cash and engineering capacity separately. | Is the alternative economically better than an optimized incumbent? |
| **Human effort per accepted change** | Total active maker, engineer, reviewer and security hours ÷ changes accepted against agreed requirements. Include failed attempts, rework, release work and post-release defects; compare changes of similar complexity. Use tickets, PRs and time logs. | Does Devin free scarce capacity after review and maintenance? Pair with elapsed request-to-release time. |
| **On-time operational completion, with quality** | On-time completions from a defined due cohort ÷ all eligible items in that same cohort, including overdue and unresolved items. Use authoritative events; report independently sampled correctness separately. Claim a fully verified correct-completion rate only with complete adjudication. Report KYC, refunds and flags separately. | Does the tool preserve or improve customer and operator outcomes? |

Use cost per correctly completed operation as a secondary efficiency measure, within each workflow and at comparable volume; disclose how correctness was assessed and any sampling uncertainty. Do not combine a KYC decision, a refund and a flag change into an unweighted “operations” count. A cheaper console that increases rework or delays onboarding can lose more value than it saves.

Two guardrails accompany the measures: **correct authority and complete evidence for privileged actions**, and **no deterioration against agreed reliability, recovery and decision-quality requirements**. A denied unauthorized request is a successful control, not a defect; an unauthorized successful action, duplicate refund or unexplained state change is a failed gate. Zero failures in a small test suite does not establish a production failure rate.

Record baseline volume, backlog age, active handling time and end-to-end p50/p95 by workflow. Keep pending and abandoned work visible. For onboarding, faster review may improve activation, but revenue impact cannot be asserted without measuring that relationship. Compliance and operations owners must define acceptable decision quality and the service windows; this research does not invent them.

### Limits that matter to customer outcomes

**Queue completeness:** a nondelegable canvas query processes the first 500 records by default, configurable to 2,000. That can silently omit matching cases; it is not a universal 2,000-row database limit. Test the actual filter, sort, connector and search against realistic queue sizes. The KPI at risk is correctly processing all eligible cases, including old cases outside the first page. [Delegation documentation][M13]

**Evidence integrity:** Dataverse auditing must be configured, retention affects what remains available, and privileged users can delete audit history. A history screen alone does not establish immutable evidence. The custom alternative also needs independent retention and access controls; an append-only application table is not sufficient against a privileged database operator. [Audit management][M11]

**Governance coverage:** current advanced connector policies add action-level controls, but have connector and rollout limitations. Custom/HTTP connectors still need the applicable classic-policy approach; do not equate one policy setting with universal business authorization. [Advanced connector policies][M14]

### Reconciling the $250K premise

At $20 × 12, $250K is approximately **1,042 equivalent Premium user-years** before other costs or discounts. This is a reconciliation clue, not evidence of 1,042 employees. Sixty engineers could support a much larger operator population; alternatively, much of the invoice may be something other than base app seats.

Ask Procurement to bridge the full invoice into:

| Cost component | Evidence needed | What migration might actually avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User/app licenses | SKU, quantity, assigned/active users, overlap and purchasing channel. | Seats or passes that can be cancelled at the relevant term. |
| Dataverse capacity | Allocated/used database, file and log capacity, growth and retention. | Only capacity no longer needed after operational and historical data exit. |
| Automation | Flow inventory, in-context versus independent flows, process/RPA licenses. | Only licenses for retired work; shared flows may remain. |
| Other Microsoft or provider services | Azure, SQL, identity, Power BI, support and source-provider fees. | Often still needed by either architecture. |

| Contract commitments | Renewal, notice period, minimums, bundle allocations and early-exit terms. | Cash only when the commitment actually falls away. |

**Do not allocate $250K ÷ 3 and claim each migrated app saves $83K.** With per-user unlimited apps, retiring two apps while retaining one used by the same people can leave the base license cost unchanged. Tenant capacity and shared automation can be similarly stranded. Compare replacement with an **optimized, equivalent-scope incumbent**, not only today’s headline bill.

Power Apps licenses provide limited in-context Power Automate rights; independent automation can need its own license. Public Power Automate prices include Premium $15/user/month, Process $150/bot/month and Hosted Process $215/bot/month under annual billing. Dataverse distinguishes database, file and log storage, with pooled subscription allowances and additional-capacity or PAYG rules. KYC files, audit retention and transaction volume affect different meters. Calculate **chargeable overage after included capacity**, not total stored GB × an add-on rate. A capacity quote belongs beside the invoice reconciliation; it is not inferred from the app count. [Capacity guidance][M18], [PAYG meters][M16]

Request entitlements and service-protection throttles are also distinct from app seats. ### Budget usage from accepted work

For a pilot, record the actual plan, opening and closing billable usage, retries, paid reviews, failed tasks and human review time. Separate prepaid credit purchases from credits consumed so the same spend is not counted twice. A useful annual model is:

> Fixed plan/contract cost + chargeable usage for accepted changes + usage spent on failures/retries + maintenance/review automation + any separate development infrastructure.

Estimate workload by type—new tool, routine change, integration change, dependency/security update—and apply measured consumption distributions. Maintain a budget cap and plan-specific spend controls. ## 5. Build economics: show the threshold and the downside

### Compare equivalent scope and incremental costs

Let **S** be annual incumbent spend actually avoidable, **B** one-time incremental build/migration/transition cost, and **R** annual incremental replacement ownership cost relative to retaining the platform. R includes changes in human administration and maintenance, hosting/database, backups/logging, identity/security tooling, Devin usage, support and review. Costs already present in both options cancel; newly increased use does not.

For a steady three-year period with savings from the start:

> **Three-year net benefit = 3S − B − 3R**
>
> **Payback after full savings start = B ÷ (S − R)**, only when S > R.
>
> **Three-year break-even recurring cost = S − B/3.**

These formulas omit discounting, taxes and unquantified business benefits. For real renewal timing use **Σ(Sₜ − Rₜ) − ΣBₜ**, with actual periods and any dual-running costs represented once. Positive cash and positive economic value are different tests: already-paid engineering time consumes capacity, but is not automatically an additional cash outlay or a headcount saving.

### Sensitivity cases, not estimates or quotations

| Scenario | S annually | B once | R annually | Three-year net benefit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Existing services and useful shared foundation | $250K | $100K | $100K | **+$350K** |
| Higher build and recurring burden | $250K | $200K | $200K | **−$50K** |
| Only part of the headline bill avoidable | $100K | $100K | $100K | **−$100K** |
| One incremental engineer-year plus $40K other recurring cost | $250K | $150K | $290K | **−$270K** |

Every numerical input in this table is an **analytical assumption**. The last case assumes $250K loaded labor per engineer-year; it is not a salary survey. No scenario is assigned a probability, and the loss cases are not a finite “worst case.” Incidents, uncapped integration work or contractual obligations can make outcomes worse.

In the first case, payback is eight months **after full savings begin**. If a three-year evaluation window loses its first year of savings but still incurs B = $100K and R = $100K each year, its net falls from +$350K to **+$100K**. Savings recognition cannot start when the prototype works if the license commitment continues.

### Annual renewal, runway and transition funding

The interview describes Microsoft licensing as yearly rolling. Use an annual-renewal scenario for planning, then verify the agreement: renewal flexibility does not establish mid-term cancellation, refunds or the ability to reduce a bundled commitment. Microsoft's published pricing announcement protects existing prices through the current term; it does not establish this client's cancellation rights. [Interview evidence][I1], [Microsoft announcement][M2]

At a deliberately flat $250K/year, three years of the stated platform bill is **$750K**. That is a reference baseline, not a forecast or a promise of $750K savings; actual prices, retained services and operating costs change it. The phrase “three years run away” is ambiguous, so the three-year model remains an analysis horizon, not a claim that the company has three years of cash runway.

Fund **one-time transition** and **recurring ownership** separately. Include initial operator/maintainer training, internal coordination and any Cognition enablement/delivery fee in B; include refresher training, agent usage and any retained support in R. Record each cost once. Evaluate maximum transition cash required alongside economic payback: a positive three-year result can still consume cash during a year of Microsoft and replacement overlap. Finance must confirm the actual funding constraint.

For internal versus assisted delivery, use the same S/B/R model for each option. Assistance can be worthwhile if lower internal build effort or earlier realized benefits exceed its fees, coordination and later support burden. Earlier delivery earns a saving only when it changes the contractual exit date or a measured business outcome. “No permanent hire” and “one and done” do not make internal maintenance free.

### The recurring engineering-capacity limit

Assume $250K loaded cost per engineer-year and $40K annual incremental non-labor cost, solely to make the threshold visible:

| Avoidable spend | Maximum incremental recurring labor before one-time cost | With $100K one-time cost recovered within three years |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $250K/year | **0.84 engineer-years/year** | **0.71 engineer-years/year** |
| $100K/year | **0.24 engineer-years/year** | **0.11 engineer-years/year** |

Calculation: `(S − $40K) / $250K`; the last column also subtracts `$100K / 3` from the numerator. The scope is **incremental portfolio labor**, including review, incident handling, shared-library changes and ten future tools, minus incumbent administration displaced. This is an economic capacity ceiling, not a staffing plan.

If verified optimization reduced the incumbent to something near the 60-user $14.4K base-license example, $40K replacement non-labor cost would already exceed that base saving before labor. Other avoidable components might change this, which is why invoice reconciliation precedes the verdict.

### How to form a defensible downside estimate

Get bounded quotes and owner estimates for migration, shared controls, each integration, production prerequisites, dual running and recurring support. Stress lower avoidability, later renewal savings, slower review, larger data retention and shared-component regression effort. Show what happens if one high-value app must remain on Microsoft. Do not assume that all 13 tools are alike or that one person can indefinitely operate them in spare time.

Keep a separate cash view and capacity view. Name the product roadmap work displaced; do not add a speculative revenue opportunity to the same engineer salary already counted in R. A business benefit such as faster refunds belongs in the model only after it has a defined baseline and supported valuation.

## 6. Test the actual workflows, not three generic CRUD screens

The following are **proposed designs and failure experiments**, not descriptions of the client’s implementation or universal regulatory requirements. Existing authoritative services should retain their rules wherever practical.

### KYC review queue

The queue is a controlled decision interface. Establish the reviewer’s authority, evidence version, policy version, allowed transition and accepted outcome. Keep identity documents in the authoritative evidence store where practical; enforce document access at the server. Identity verification and sanctions screening remain separate dependencies unless deliberately included in scope.

- **Outcome:** correct decisions within the agreed review interval; diagnose with overdue-case rate and p95 case age, not only completed-case averages.
- **Quality guardrail:** independent re-review/disagreement and unauthorized or stale transitions; faster approval is not success if decision quality deteriorates.
- **Failure experiment:** two reviewers load the same case. After one commits, the second submits an incompatible stale decision. Reject it without overwriting history; record the accepted actor, evidence and reason.
- **Scope question:** can an existing provider’s dashboard serve the need without a new wrapper? Stripe Identity explicitly documents a controlled-access Dashboard option; that is an example, not an assumed vendor. [Identity review options][W1]

This is a parity requirement, not a unique custom-code benefit: Dataverse already supports conditional updates using ETags and `If-Match`. Test whether the existing workflow actually uses them. [Conditional operations][W6]

### Refunds dashboard

First distinguish reporting, approval and actual payment execution. A read-only dashboard has different requirements from an endpoint that moves money: measure correct enquiry resolution and data freshness for reporting-only scope. For execution workflows, separate internal request/approval/submission from the actual provider lifecycle, including pending, requires-action, succeeded, failed and canceled states where applicable. Stripe also supports partial refunds bounded by the original amount; the customer’s provider may differ. [Refund lifecycle][W2]

- **Outcome:** correct refunds reaching the required final state within the service window; track pending age and reconciliation exceptions. Provider settlement time should be distinguished from internal handling delay.
- **Financial guardrail:** duplicate/over-limit refund amount, unauthorized execution and unresolved mismatches with the authoritative ledger/provider.
- **Failure experiment:** the provider accepts a refund and the request times out. A retry must preserve the business operation identity and reconcile uncertainty before issuing another operation. Provider idempotency is useful, but has a scope and retention period; it is not the complete ledger. [Idempotent requests][W3]
- **Architecture implication:** a database transaction cannot atomically commit a remote payment. Persist durable intent, use controlled retries, verify webhook events and reconcile accepted outcomes. Test concurrent partial refunds at the authoritative boundary.

Stripe documents duplicate and out-of-order webhook delivery. Verify signatures, deduplicate processing and test reordered events. A provider success status also need not mean the customer has already observed a bank credit. These distinctions protect reconciliation quality and honest customer communication. [Webhook guidance][W5], [refund lifecycle][W2]

### Feature-flag admin panel

Separate the administration interface from flag evaluation, targeting, distribution, defaults and caching. OpenFeature defines the evaluation/provider contract; it is not a flag management backend. Preserve an existing flag service where useful. [Provider specification][W4]

- **Outcome:** authorized changes correctly observed in the intended environment within an agreed interval; compare desired and observed versions.
- **Guardrail:** wrong-environment changes, overly broad targeting and incidents/rollback after a change. A toggle that bypasses KYC can have greater risk than its UI suggests.
- **Failure experiment:** the admin save succeeds, but a production consumer still serves the old version. Show propagation state and last observation rather than equating a database update with successful rollout.
- **Scope question:** does the existing flag platform already provide the required administration, approval and history?

The reusable pattern is **identity → policy → validated command → authoritative state change → evidence and reconciliation**. Generic “update any field” APIs can bypass the invariants that make these workflows safe.

## 7. Architecture and options for thirteen tools

### A plausible owned-software boundary

Reuse the client’s identity provider, hosting, secrets system, CI/CD and observability if they are already suitable. Start with one application foundation and explicit workflow modules. The browser calls a server that verifies identity and permissions for each command; narrow adapters call existing KYC, payments and flag services. Store workflow metadata, durable intents and operation history; minimize copied sensitive evidence.

Commands such as `request_refund` and `approve_case` are easier to review than unconstrained status updates. Shared events can record actor, authority, action, reason, record/operation ID, previous/new version, policy version, timestamp and correlation ID. Use separate credentials and privileges for read-only analytics and financial execution. A single broad “admin” role is insufficient when different operations have different authority.

For a prototype a modular monolith reduces moving parts. Later isolation can follow privilege and reliability needs. A shared foundation reduces repeated work but creates correlated risk: an authorization defect or schema migration can affect every module. Budget for cross-module regression, dependency updates, onboarding, documentation and a second maintainer. This is a design proposal, not a production architecture validated by the research.

### Customization must remain useful after handover

Choose representative business changes with operations: a revised KYC routing/policy rule, a refund-approval threshold or a flag-targeting rule. Measure human effort and elapsed lead time through reviewed release and operator acceptance. Preserve the underlying authorization and state invariants. A configurable workflow is useful only if it fits the actual process; unrestricted customization can also increase testing and support obligations.

Prefer technology the owning team can operate. Training must cover maintainers and operators, including who can change what and how failures are handled. Record initial instruction in B and ongoing onboarding/retraining in R. Verify whether custom code transfers routine changes from business makers to engineers; that can consume capacity even if the new UI is better.

Before production handover, propose an **independent maintenance exercise**: a designated maintainer who did not author the relevant module makes a representative change, runs checks, deploys and rehearses recovery in an approved test environment using the repo and docs. Name a primary and backup owner with allocated capacity. Track builder interventions and unresolved gaps. Confirm client control of source, deployment accounts, secrets, runbooks and incident escalation, and test withdrawal of temporary development access. An application that needs its original builder for every change has not demonstrated the intended ownership transfer. [Detailed proposed acceptance tests][I1]

### Code apps: a real third path with limits

Power Apps code apps allow custom code/frameworks while the platform supplies hosting, Entra integration, connector access and managed policies. Devin could help author such code, subject to testing SDK compatibility, build and deployment. Current licensing options are Premium, PAYG, App Pass or auto-claim; maker and end-user requirements must be verified. [Code apps overview][M4]

The same overview lists no Secure Implicit Connections and no Power Platform Git integration for code apps. Source code can still live in ordinary Git; that is different from the platform’s Git feature. Assets have a public endpoint without IP restrictions; Microsoft points to Entra Conditional Access for access control. Test the actual authentication and data path. This route could solve customization constraints while preserving platform operations, but is not a license-exit strategy.

Code apps are client-side single-page applications, not arbitrary hosting for a generated server. A custom command backend may still need separate infrastructure. Test the connector/authentication boundary and publish through supported solution/ALM mechanisms. [Architecture][M26], [code-app ALM][M27]

### Cognition assistance is a delivery choice, not a runtime platform

Make three decisions separately: **where the apps run, who builds them, and who operates them afterward**. Devin can assist internal delivery or an agreed external engagement; neither arrangement automatically determines whether Power Apps is retained.

| Delivery model | Potential benefit | Commitment that remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal team using Devin | Work stays close to existing systems and domain owners. | Fund specification, review, training, delivery and continuing operations. |
| Scoped Cognition assistance, if offered, followed by internal ownership | May add initial enablement/delivery capacity and avoid permanent hiring for the build peak. | Obtain a quote and acceptance scope; reserve client review time and demonstrate transfer to funded maintainers. |
| Continuing assistance/support, if offered and contracted | May address a continuing skills or capacity gap. | Add its actual fees and dependencies to recurring cost; specify escalation, response and exit arrangements. |

Cognition publicly documents forward-deployed engineers supporting project selection, enablement and ROI measurement in a specific enterprise deployment. That demonstrates such assistance exists in some engagements, not that this fintech receives a four-person team, a start within two days, finished-app delivery or continuing maintenance with its subscription. Obtain the actual offer. [Cognition deployment example][D14]

Separate time to access/configuration, first accepted PR and first operationally accepted release. Repository access within days does not establish that integration, security review, migration or training has finished. Clarify whether Cognition is helping the client adopt Devin, delivering named applications, or taking a contracted support role; these are different deliverables.

### Concentration and continuity

The interview's vendor-stability concern is an operating question: which failures or commercial changes could interrupt the workflow, and how can the client recover or exit? No vendor solvency assessment was supplied. An owned app may still depend on Microsoft identity, cloud or data services, so replacing Power Apps need not remove Microsoft concentration. It can also introduce dependence on one maintainer or an external delivery team.

Inventory runtime, data, identity, development and human dependencies separately. Test exports, source/build reproducibility, restore and maintainer continuity. Require an exit route appropriate to the risk; adding more vendors is not by itself evidence of better resilience. Keep the runtime independent of development-agent availability, as proposed above. [Interview interpretation][I1]

### Compare more than “keep the bill” versus “replace everything”

| Option | When it is plausible | KPI and commercial test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimize/keep Power Apps | Existing governance and maker workflow are valuable. | Reconciled avoidable spend, utilization and baseline change effort after rightsizing. |
| Devin + owned applications | Mature APIs/infrastructure and funded ownership; substantial real license exit. | Net benefit survives downside staffing and migration assumptions; accepted changes need less human effort. |
| Devin + Power Apps code apps | Customization is the main problem; managed runtime is useful. | Improved change effort/UX at retained platform cost; compatibility proven. |
| Selective migration | Some workflows fit custom code better. | Actual cash savings or measurable business improvement justify dual operations and stranded licenses. |
| Another managed internal-tool platform | Requirements are ordinary and price is the primary concern. | Equivalent controls and a current quote beat both optimized Microsoft and custom ownership. |
| Remove an unnecessary wrapper | A provider’s existing console already satisfies the workflow. | Equivalent operator outcomes without another application to maintain. |

Two public alternatives provide price anchors, **not migration recommendations**:

- **Retool:** annual-billed Team is $10/builder + $5/internal user monthly; Business $50 + $15. Business includes audit logs and richer permissions. Custom SSO, SCIM, Git source control and self-hosting are Enterprise features requiring a quote. A purely illustrative Business population of 5 builders + 55 other internal users gives $12,900/year before usage/add-ons; it is not a fintech-equivalent all-in offer. [Current Retool pricing][A1]
- **Appsmith:** Business is $15/user/month, up to 99 users; its user count includes makers and assigned members, with no extra developer-seat fee. Enterprise starts at $2,500/month for 100 users and adds SAML/OIDC, SCIM and CI/CD; managed hosting/air-gapped options can add cost. Sixty Business users calculate to $10,800/year, while the published Enterprise starting package annualizes to $30,000. Required controls, hosting and migration must be quoted. [Current Appsmith pricing][A2]

Low public prices do not demonstrate that the existing $250K is waste: the actual user population, contractual bundle and required operating controls remain unknown. They do demonstrate that renegotiation and alternative procurement deserve a place beside the custom-build hypothesis.

## 8. Migration and evidence retention are separate work

An application can be easy to regenerate while the system is expensive to leave. Inventory apps, flows, connectors, service accounts, roles, documents, data ownership, business rules, history and consumers before scoping a rewrite.

Microsoft’s solution/pipeline mechanisms move application artifacts, not Dataverse business rows. Separate data migration, relationship preservation and reconciliation are required. Dataverse backups cannot simply be downloaded as an offline database; restore behavior and audit-history inclusion also need explicit checking. Default restore behavior can exclude audit logs. Do not assume that an exported solution or database restore preserves the decision evidence needed after retirement. [Solution concepts][M20], [pipelines][M10], [backup/restore][M12]

Managed-solution uninstall can remove associated data in custom tables/columns. Rehearse exports and validate recoverability before destructive cleanup. [Solution removal][M21]

A cutover plan should establish one authoritative writer per workflow, reconcile old/new reads and maintain historical evidence access. For money-moving actions, restoring a database cannot undo an external payment. Rollback must account for actions already executed at the provider. Measure reconciliation exceptions, evidence coverage, cutover interruption and support load as transition costs and outcome guardrails.

## 9. A bounded prototype that answers a customer question

This is a planned experiment, **not a build performed here**. Samuel subsequently selected a portal spanning all three tools, with insurance-informed synthetic examples and a hard two-hour build cap. This supersedes the earlier research proposal for one deep KYC slice and an optional second module. The [current plan](<../2. Planning/0.prototype-plan.md>) and [decision log, D006 and D017–D022](<../2. Planning/1.decision-log.md>) are the handoff authority for that scope.

Use a fictional insurer/counterparty review as the KYC proxy, a claim/payment request with simulated approval as the refund proxy, and one sandbox flag update. Keep their actual behavior thin and reuse identity, server permissions, persistent history and linked counterparty/claim records. The proxy does not establish the fintech's actual requirements or full KYC compliance. Code apps remain a documentation-backed hybrid comparison rather than a second build.

| Elapsed time | Work and evidence sought |
| --- | --- |
| 0–15 min | Confirm Devin Cloud access, current scope and run path; record setup friction. |
| 15–45 min | Shared demo identity, permissions, persisted data, audit and portal shell. |
| 45–80 min | Three thin module actions using the same control/history foundation and linked records. |
| 80–105 min | Test denied requests, stale/invalid writes, persistence and shared behavior; fix material failures. |
| 105–120 min | Run documentation, module guide and evidence; stop at the cap and report gaps. |

A role dropdown is a permission simulation, not demonstrated authentication/SSO. A local audit table is not demonstrated tamper-resistant retention. A second menu item is not demonstrated reusable infrastructure. Label each conclusion as **demonstrated**, **documented product capability**, or **untested**.

Record wall-clock time separately from active human time, usage and retries. Add a small change request if time permits; modification/review effort is more informative than initial screen generation. Longer-term availability, incident rate, sustained maintenance and full production-control equivalence cannot be established in two hours.

The interview adds a useful bounded check: if time permits, have someone other than the original builder run a small documented change and record required help. Otherwise list knowledge transfer as untested. The full maintenance/recovery acceptance exercise belongs to a funded production-readiness phase; it is not a reason to exceed the assessment's two-hour build limit.

Independent productivity studies reinforce the need for local measurement. A Google randomized study of 96 engineers estimated about 21% shorter task time with its tested tooling, with substantial uncertainty and limited transferability. METR’s early-2025 experiment with 16 experienced developers and 246 tasks found 19% longer task time with the tested tools. Neither measured current Devin in this use case. [Google study][R1], [METR study][R2]

METR’s February 2026 update reported indications of improved speed but judged the new estimates unreliable because of participation/task selection and timing problems. Do not use the old slowdown as a current universal verdict or the later raw estimates as a Devin forecast. [METR update][R3]

## 10. Evidence that would change the recommendation

| Priority question | Evidence needed | Effect on the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much of $250K can disappear, and when? | Invoice/order form, channel, renewal, active/assigned users, app overlap and minimum commitments. | Establishes S and whether a selective move saves cash. |
| What work does the platform already eliminate? | Recent change tickets, maker/engineer time, admin workload, incidents and support contracts. | Establishes the true baseline for R and capacity consumption. |
| What do the ten planned tools share? | Backlog grouped by users, integrations, data, permission and command patterns. | Establishes whether a shared foundation is useful or ten unrelated systems are being created. |
| Which system owns each rule and action? | App/flow/service dependency map and production behavior. | Separates a UI replacement from a business-system migration. |
| What outcomes and controls must be preserved? | Operations/compliance/security definitions, baseline quality and latency, recovery expectations. | Sets acceptance gates without invented regulatory requirements. |
| Who owns production and changes? | Named team, capacity, backup maintainer, escalation and budgets. | Makes recurring ownership explicit. |
| What can Devin actually cost and access? | Plan/quote, usage logs, resolved Teams discrepancy, signed data terms and permitted development data. | Establishes incremental dev cost and usable workflow. |
| Are skills or available hours the constraint? | Skills by named owner, build/review queues, roadmap commitments and funded recurring hours. | Distinguishes execution assistance from a continuing ownership gap. |
| Does the tool fit the actual business changes? | Operations-defined examples, acceptance results, workarounds and maker/maintainer training time. | Makes customization and knowledge transfer measurable. |
| What would Cognition assistance actually include? | Confirmed staffing/availability, scoped deliverables, fees, client effort, handover and support/exit terms. | Tests the interview's capacity hypothesis without inventing an included service. |
| Can the team afford and complete the transition before renewal? | Notice deadline, overlap cash budget, actual runway/funding constraint and migration/handover schedule. | Connects annual flexibility to a feasible savings date. |

**Proceed toward owned tools only when** avoidable spend and exit timing are evidenced, controls have an owner, downside economics remain acceptable, and a measured workflow shows useful accepted functionality per human hour. **Retain or optimize managed tooling** when its maker/governance value is high, user overlap makes exit savings small, or recurring ownership exceeds the threshold. A selective migration can still be justified by a measured operational improvement even when it saves no license cash; present that as an explicit business case.

The interview makes a **scoped Cognition-assisted build with internal maintenance** a candidate when initial delivery capacity is constrained but long-term ownership is genuinely funded. If review and continuing maintenance are also unfunded, completing the build externally does not resolve that constraint. These conditions should change the delivery recommendation; they do not, by themselves, settle the platform choice.

The next decision is therefore whether to run the bounded experiment and obtain the commercial inventory—not whether a two-hour demo proves that a fintech can safely replace its platform.
